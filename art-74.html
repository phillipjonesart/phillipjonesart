Work

Art 74

Art 74 is a new media art class I took at San José State University in Fall 2026. Every few weeks the class moved to a new digital form: glitch art, memes, video mashups, hypertext, and finally this website.

These four pieces are my answers to those prompts. Each one takes something familiar, like a famous painting, a kids' show, home video next to Cold War footage, or a Greek myth, and bends it into something new.

Projects: Glitch · 2D Meme · Time-Based Mashup · Hypertext

Glitch

Felt Hat, Corrupted

2026 · Glitch art, digital image

For the glitch project I took Vincent van Gogh's Self-Portrait with Grey Felt Hat (1887) and broke its digital file on purpose, letting the errors slice the painting into shifted bands of blue and violet. The second version goes further. The face turns gray and mirrors into a repeating pattern until it almost stops being a portrait.

Van Gogh's brushstrokes already look a lot like texture and noise. I wanted to see how much of him survives when the data underneath the image falls apart.

2D Meme

Can We Just Talk About the Political and Economic State of the World Right Now

2026 · 2D meme, digital collage

For the meme project I made a collage in Photoshop. Four world leaders' faces are pasted onto the Teletubbies, who wave from the famous green-hill desktop wallpaper while the Twin Towers burn behind them and a grinning sun looks on.

The title is the thing people say when the news gets to be too much. I used the brightest, most childish images I could find to show how strange it feels to watch real disaster and real power play out like a kids' show.

Time-Based Mashup

Test Site

2026 · Time-based mashup, video, 53 seconds

For the time-based mashup I cut back and forth every half second between two videos: a white puppy chasing a toy around a backyard, and archival color footage of a Cold War atomic bomb test in the Nevada desert, filmed through the branches of a Joshua tree. The bomb footage sits on top of the backyard like a window, so the puppy's chase and the slowly rising mushroom cloud play out at the same time.

The flicker is the point. It's how a phone feed feels, with something adorable right next to something horrifying and both getting the same half second of attention. The backyard and the test site are both American scenes, and the video asks how we hold them in our heads together.

Hypertext

Push

2026 · Hypertext, HTML

Push is a ten-page hypertext that turns the viewer into Sisyphus, the king in Greek myth who was condemned to roll a boulder up a hill forever. Each page is drawn only with slashes and the letter O. The O is the boulder, and it is also the only link. To move forward you have to click the boulder, and every click pushes it a little higher.

The menu lists all ten pages, but only the next one works, so you can't skip ahead. On the tenth page the boulder rolls back to the bottom and you have to start again. I built it by hand in HTML. It asks whether endless effort can still mean something, or, as Albert Camus wrote, whether we have to imagine Sisyphus happy.
