Work

Space

I keep coming back to space, especially the early years of the space race, when every flight was a risk and the suits and machines were built by hand. These pieces mix painting, character design, and storyboarding. I made them on my own time because the history and the design of that era fascinate me.

Mercury Astronaut

2026 · Digital painting

A painted portrait of an astronaut from Project Mercury, the first U.S. crewed spaceflights in the early 1960s. I kept the suit loose and blocky and spent more care on the face, to get the feeling of an old press photo. I wanted him to look calm and a little tired, like a real person instead of a poster.

Mercury Spacesuit Turnaround

2026 · Digital drawing, character design sheet

One figure shown in three layers: in his underwear, in the suit's inner pressure layer and hoses, and in the full silver suit and helmet. Animation studios use sheets like this to keep a character consistent. Building one made me figure out how the real suit actually goes together.

Liftoff

2026 · Digital ink, storyboard frame

A black and white storyboard frame of an astronaut pinned back in his seat during launch, gripping the controls. The lines of the cockpit window all pull toward him. I drew it to practice dramatic camera angles and to get the weight of a launch across in a single frame.

Spacesuit Study

2026 · Digital drawing

A full-body design of a spacewalk suit, drawn straight on against black. I focused on the panels, hoses, patches, and wrinkles that make a suit read as real equipment and not a costume.

Tranquility

2026 · Digital painting

A lunar landscape with the landing module on the left, a tiny astronaut in the distance, and Earth hanging in a black sky. I kept the astronaut small on purpose so the size of the empty surface does the work.
